the physics

What you are looking at

D ∇⁴w = ρh ω² w,  D = Eh³ / 12(1 − ν²)

Each solution on a plate free at every edge is a mode w(x, y) with its own frequency. The sand settles on its nodal set, the curves where w = 0 and the plate does not move.

The plate

Each outline is solved with Argyris triangles, quintic elements with continuous slopes. Your browser receives each mode as the exact quintic on every triangle, and evaluates it per pixel for the lines and fringes and on a grid for the sand.

Metal and size only rescale the frequency, f = Ω √(D/ρh) / 2πL². Poisson's ratio stays inside the equation, so each metal is solved on its own. A note shared by two figures is drawn as twin stones, and moving the bow along the edge turns its figure.

The sand

Where the plate moves, grains are thrown and land at random. Where it is still, they stay. That walk has no drift,

dx = √(2D dt) ξ,  D ∝ D₀ + |A(x)|

and its steady state is ρ ∝ 1/D, which is the nodal set. Abramian, Protière, Lazarus and Devauchelle measured this in 2025.

The sound

Each ear gets its own Rayleigh integral over the vibrating surface, so turning the plate changes what you hear. A bowed note is a pure tone that rings down at π η f plus its radiation loss. A tap is every mode at once, struck with a felt mallet.

Three ways of looking

Sand. Chladni's way. Stroboscope. The motion slowed to one swing every two seconds and exaggerated, lighter where the sheet is above its rest. Hologram. A time-averaged hologram glows as J₀²(4πA/λ), with a dark fringe at every sixth of a micron of swing, shown in black and white as the first ones were. Nodal lines. The exact zero set of the solution.
